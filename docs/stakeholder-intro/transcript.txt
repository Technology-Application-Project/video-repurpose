[00:00] Brevio Studio
Brevio Studio turns one long lecture into a short, reviewed video, for the audience you choose.

[00:06] The problem
Lectures hold great ideas, but few people watch an hour of video. Cutting highlights by hand is slow, and fully automatic tools are hard to trust.

[00:16] Step 1 · Source
You start with a lecture video and pick the audience: researchers, the general public, or kids. A source link is optional.

[00:24] Step 2 · Clips
Brevio transcribes the lecture on your own computer and suggests the strongest clips. You keep up to five and fine tune each one.

[00:33] Step 2 · Blueprint
Before writing anything, it plans the story: clip count, opening idea, bridges and ending. The finished video is checked against that plan.

[00:42] Step 3 · Studio
It builds a preview with a narrated opening, bridges and ending, plus illustrations, music and captions, each section beside its source evidence.

[00:51] Step 3 · Approval
Nothing is exported until a named reviewer has watched the preview and approved it.

[00:57] Step 4 · Export
Every export gets two plain-language checks: technical, for media, captions and loudness, and structure, for order, bridges, narration and the plan.

[01:07] Safeguards
Transcription, voices and rendering run locally. Only short text requests use the company gateway, capped at five per run. A person always decides.

[01:17] Brevio Studio
Faster highlights, consistent quality, and a human in control. Try it on one lecture, and tell us what to improve.
